FAQ

Straight answers.

The things parents actually ask, answered plainly — including the awkward ones.

Is this spyware? Are you watching my child?#

No — and it can't be, by design. Kintrinsic never records your child's location, never uploads their browsing history, and never keeps a profile of them. The only things it reports are numbers: time used, requests made. Never what they typed, said, or looked at.

It's a fence with a gate you hold, not a camera in the corner.

Wasn't this called Charter?#

Yes — Kintrinsic is the new name for the product that launched as Charter. Same product, same people, same promises; nothing about how it works or what it refuses to collect has changed.

Everything you install now carries the Kintrinsic name — the phone app, the computer installer and its menu entries. Only the command you'd type in a terminal (charterd) keeps the name charter, as a permanent internal name. Installing over the top keeps your key, your family and your limits.

One nice side effect: “charter” now clearly means the thing it always described — the agreement your family writes — rather than doubling as a brand.

What data do you collect about my family?#

Essentially none. There's no account to create, and no server of ours holding your family's life. Your rules are sealed by a key that only you hold, on your phone, and carried over an encrypted relay that can't read or forge them. We couldn't hand over your data to anyone — because we don't have it.

Is it free? What's the catch?#

It's free while in early access. There's no catch of the usual kind — no data to sell, no ads, no subscription that quietly profiles your child. Kintrinsic is part of the Signet project, built on the idea that families should own their own keys.

Which devices does it work on?#

Today: Linux computers — Linux Mint 22.x and other Ubuntu-based systems. This is the proven, ready path.

In preview: de-Googled Android phones (a private, Google-free Android such as GrapheneOS on a Pixel).

Not yet: Windows, macOS, or iPhone/iPad. Apple doesn't allow the kind of on-device control Kintrinsic needs, so an iPhone can't be managed this way. See the roadmap for what's coming.

Does it work without the internet?#

Yes. The rules live on the device itself, signed by your key — so the schedule holds on a train, in a caravan, or with the router unplugged. Anything your child asks for while offline simply catches up the next time it reconnects.

Can my child just get around it?#

Honest answer: Kintrinsic is built for a cooperative home, not an arms race. It comfortably resists the casual stuff — closing it, changing the clock, poking at settings. A determined, technical teenager with full physical access to a computer can eventually find ways around almost anything, and we won't pretend otherwise.

The design leans on trust and gentle friction, with the family agreement doing the real work. If the relationship is adversarial, no app fixes that — but Kintrinsic won't make it worse by spying.

What if I lose my phone, or my key?#

Your child's limits keep working either way — they live on the device. To manage again, you restore your key from the encrypted backup Kintrinsic prompts you to save during setup, then reconnect. The computer also has an admin-only Recovery screen in the Kintrinsic app as a way back in. The one thing to do is take that backup and keep it safe.

Do I need to be technical?#

For the Linux computer: if you can install an app and use a settings menu, you can set up Kintrinsic — it's a double-click and a couple of choices, working within minutes.

If you'd like a hand, the Kintrinsic app has a “Copy this guide for your AI” button. It gives you a plain-text runbook to paste into ChatGPT or Claude, which will then walk you through your own setup one step at a time and answer questions as they come up.

The de-Googled phone is more involved (it's in preview), because a phone has to be handed control in a specific way. The phone setup guide walks through it step by step.

What can I actually set?#

Allowed hours, a daily time limit with gentle warnings, specific games and apps (block one, or give it its own hours), website filtering, and “learning is free” so homework doesn't cost playtime. You can also name what time is for in your own words — “Play” gets an hour a day, schoolwork costs nothing. Your child can ask for more time from the lock screen, and you approve with a tap. Is it open source?#

Yes. Kintrinsic is published under the MIT licence — on GitHub, because that's where people will look first, and heading for a decentralised home that isn't owned by Microsoft.

Every download comes with checksums, so that “we can't read your data” stops being our word for it and becomes something you can check yourself. That's the point of doing it, and it's why we did it properly rather than quickly.

The roadmap tracks where it's up to.

Why “wardship” and not “parental controls”?#

Because the words shape the thing. “Parental controls” frames a child as something to be monitored. Kintrinsic is a guardian holding a charter for a ward — an agreement you keep on their behalf, not surveillance you run against them. Same job; a kinder frame.
