Roadmap

What's real, and what's next.

Kintrinsic is early, and we'd rather tell you exactly where the line is than blur it. Here's what's proven on real family hardware, what's freshly landed, and what's still ahead — no vapourware.

live

Shipped & proven on hardware

Running on real family computers, every day. If you install Kintrinsic tonight, this is what you get — and it's a complete setup on its own, with no phone and no internet required.

Kintrinsic for Linux

A one-package install for Mint and Ubuntu — the warden, lock screen, setup wizard and recovery app together. Double-click and it's on; nothing changes until you say so.

Allowed hours

When the computer can be used, set per day of the week, with one-off overrides for exam days and birthdays. Enforced on the device, offline included.

Daily time & warnings

A daily total, counting only time actually spent at the machine — idle, asleep and locked time is free. Ten- and one-minute warnings before it's up.

The lock screen

A calm, undismissable lock that says why it's locked, when time comes back, and offers the ask. Open apps are frozen where they stand, not killed, so nothing is lost.

Ask for more time

Your child asks in their own words from the lock screen; you grant a few minutes with one tap, and Kintrinsic routes the ask to whichever limit is actually blocking.

Website filtering

Age-appropriate browsing by site, with SafeSearch and restricted YouTube enforced at the network layer. Proven on the family Linux computer.

Learning is free

Learning apps don't count against screen time, so homework never costs playtime.

Phone pairing & remote lock

One-scan QR pairing, and remote lock and unlock — proven live on a real GrapheneOS Pixel, surviving reboots.

Encrypted key backup

Back up and restore your signing key, so losing your phone never locks you out for good.

new

Newly landed — early access

Built and merged, being verified on real devices now. Early-access families are the first through — set on the computer, these work regardless.

Per-app limits & app holds

Block a game, give it its own hours, or leave it installed but closed until they ask.

Time in your own words

Name what screen time is for — “Play” costs an hour a day, schoolwork costs nothing — daily or weekly.

Approve new apps

Your child asks to install something; you approve or decline from your phone.

Learning sites as their own apps

Khan Academy, Wikipedia, BBC Bitesize and Duolingo open locked to that one site, and cost nothing.

Time in the corner of their screen

A panel icon showing what's left, and a way to ask for more before it runs out.

Give time, and finish now

Add minutes without being asked, or call time with a minute's warning. Both clear overnight.

One budget across devices

Phone and computer share one daily total, counted honestly — an hour on both at once is an hour.

The family's week

One bar a day per device against the time you agreed. Going over is shown to talk about, not slammed.

Unlock from your phone

A short code lets them back in with no internet on either side.

The lifeline

A locked phone is still a phone: five people they can always reach, plus real emergency numbers.

The emergency unlock

A fire alarm, not a lock. They can always open their phone — and you're told the moment they do.

Web filtering & hotspot on the phone

The same age-appropriate filtering carried onto the phone, and sharing the connection stays off unless you allow it.

planned

Coming next

The new name, everywhere

Kintrinsic is the new name for what launched as Charter. Everything you see installs as Kintrinsic — the apps, the Linux package and its menu entry. Only the under-the-hood command and daemon (charter / charterd) keep the original name, deliberately and permanently — an ordinary update never touches your keys or limits.

A decentralised source home

Kintrinsic is published under the MIT licence on GitHub, with checksums for every download; next it moves to a decentralised home that isn't Microsoft's.

A ready-to-go phone

A trusted, pre-set-up refurbished phone for families who'd rather not tinker — private and managed out of the box.

More phones than Pixel

Testing Kintrinsic on other Android 14+ phones (CalyxOS, LineageOS) so a wider range of devices can join.

Recovery on the device

Stronger ways back in from the computer itself, for the times the phone isn't to hand.

Per-app polish

Finishing touches on app and game rules as early-access families put them through their paces.

exploring

Exploring (not promised)

Kintrinsic on Linux phones

Ubuntu Touch and postmarketOS could reuse the desktop warden — a genuinely open, Google-free handset.

Fully-remote setup

Signing everything from your phone with Signet, so even first setup needs no hands on the device.
