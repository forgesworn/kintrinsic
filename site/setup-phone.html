Setup guide · the phone preview

Set up a child's phone.

Longer than the computer, and more involved — about twenty minutes, most of it a factory reset running on its own. Pairing and remote lock are proven on real hardware; the rest is in preview with early-access families.

Read this bit first. Android will only hand over the control Kintrinsic needs on a phone with no accounts on it at all. That means the phone must be factory reset, and a factory reset erases everything on it. If this is a phone already in use, back it up — or better, do this on a phone you're giving them fresh.

What you'll need

  • A Pixel running GrapheneOS — a private, Google-free Android. Other Android 14+ phones are being tested but aren't supported yet.
  • A USB cable and a computer.
  • The Kintrinsic app on your own phone.

1 · Factory reset the phone

Reset it, and on first boot skip every account sign-in. Don't add a Google account, don't restore a backup, don't sign into anything. The phone has to be genuinely empty — that emptiness is what lets Kintrinsic become its device owner, and it's a one-time window that closes as soon as an account is added.

2 · Turn on USB debugging

On the phone: Settings → About phone, then tap Build number seven times. That unlocks Developer options, where you switch on USB debugging. Plug the phone into the computer and accept the prompt that appears on the phone's screen.

3 · Install and hand over control

This step runs from the computer, over the cable: a guided setup fetches the app, installs it, makes it the phone's device owner, and starts pairing. You get one prompt on the phone — Pair with guardian — and that completes it.

Being rebuilt under the new name. The guided cable setup lived in the old Charter web app, which is being retired as part of the move to Kintrinsic — the same tool is being folded into Kintrinsic itself. Until it ships, phone setup stays hands-on with early-access families, and the roadmap tracks it. The computer setup needs none of this and works today.

4 · Name it and sign the charter

Give the phone a name in the Kintrinsic app, choose Govern this device, then set its allowed hours and daily time and sign. From that moment the phone accepts rules from your key and no one else's.

If your child also has a Kintrinsic computer, the two share one daily total rather than one each — an hour on both at once counts as an hour, not two.

What still works when the phone is locked

A locked phone is still a phone. Out of hours, your child can always:

  • Reach up to five people you name, by name, plus their country's real emergency number — 999 here, 911 in the States, and it follows you abroad. Held rather than tapped, so a pocket can't dial.
  • Break the glass. An emergency unlock opens the phone immediately, with no waiting and no signal needed — and tells you the moment they do. Nothing stops them; it's a fire alarm, not a lock.
  • See the time, the battery and their signal. Being out of hours never makes those less true.

If something goes wrong

To hand the phone back completely, remove Kintrinsic as device owner from the parent app, or factory reset the phone again — a reset always works and always will. Your own key is backed up separately; losing the phone doesn't lose your ability to manage anything else.

Honestly: this is the preview. One-scan pairing and remote lock and unlock are proven on a real GrapheneOS Pixel. Web filtering on the phone, the lifeline and per-app rules are newly landed and being verified with early-access families. The roadmap is kept current.
