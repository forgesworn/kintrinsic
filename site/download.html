Download

Get Kintrinsic.

Two pieces, and you only strictly need the first: the installer for your child's computer, and the app for your own phone. No account, no store, no tracking.

Start here · your child's computer · Linux

Kintrinsic for Linux 0.7.9

One installer: the warden, the lock screen and the Kintrinsic app — where you set up, adjust limits, connect a phone and recover access, all in one place. Installing changes nothing until you set them — no limits apply until you do. This alone is a complete, working setup.

Download the installer (.deb)

64-bit · about 11 MB · then in a terminal, from your Downloads folder: sudo apt install ./the-downloaded-file.deb · or from the Blossom mirror

  • Linux Mint 22.x, on an X11 session (Cinnamon's default)
  • A standard (non-admin) account for your child
  • A separate admin account for you
  • Chromium installed, if you want learning apps
  • About five minutes — honestly

New here? The setup guide walks every step.

Optional · your phone · Android

Kintrinsic for Android 0.1.16

The parent app — for your phone, not your child's. Approve “more time”, get an alert the moment your child asks — even with the screen off — and change limits from anywhere. It talks to your family directly; there is no Google service in the middle.

Download Kintrinsic for Android

About 10 MB · your phone needs Android 10 or newer (GrapheneOS included) · or from the Blossom mirror

Already have it? Updates arrive in the app, and you tap to install. Installing over the top works too, and keeps your key and your family — nothing to set up again.

A note on the name under the hood. Everything you see says Kintrinsic — the apps, the installer, the menus. Only the terminal command and daemon (charterd), the internal app id and a few other under-the-hood names keep the earlier name charter — deliberate, permanent internal names (much like Signal's package id). Same product, same keys.
  • Your phone: Android 10 or newer, GrapheneOS included
  • No Google Play, no Google account
  • Reliable alerts with the screen off
  • Your key stays on your phone

Installing it on your phone

  1. Tap the download button above and let the file finish.
  2. Open the downloaded .apk file from your notifications or Files app.
  3. Android will ask whether to allow installing from this source. Allow it once, and the install proceeds.
Android will show you a warning, and that's normal. It appears for every app installed outside the Play Store — it is not a judgement about Kintrinsic specifically, and it is not a sign that anything is wrong. Android simply can't vouch for a file it didn't distribute.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

Because listing there would mean a Google developer account, Google's review of what your family is allowed to run, and — for the child-side app — permissions we've deliberately kept out of Google's hands. The whole point of Kintrinsic is that no company sits between you and your child's device, and that has to include us and them. So we hand you the file directly.

If your browser renames the file

Some browsers append .zip to downloads they don't recognise. If yours does, rename it so it ends in .apk and it will install normally. The file itself is unchanged.

Verifying this download (optional)

Checking a download confirms one thing: that the file you have is the file we built. It's a reasonable thing to want, and we'd rather you could. The source is public, and releases are built in public CI, so what you check against is something you can inspect too.

Android. Every release comes with a SHA256SUMS file and a GitHub build-provenance attestation. Put the two files in one folder, then:

sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
gh attestation verify kintrinsic-0.1.16.apk --repo forgesworn/kintrinsic

The Android app is signed with our release key. Its certificate's SHA-256 fingerprint is 4a783a3e2c087906bf29d4d5002b546705fa50f9d8344ec5dad088e4740cfcdc

Linux. The current package predates that pipeline, so its SHA-256 is published in the release notes and here instead:

bf2fd45b51a728879f6d0f4e8c42109181d499bd15d024987795f9f08553cbbb kintrinsic_0.7.9_amd64.deb

Save that line as a file and run sha256sum -c on it, or just compare the output of sha256sum kintrinsic_0.7.9_amd64.deb by eye. Later Linux releases carry SHA256SUMS and an attestation too.

Downloads come from GitHub releases, with a Blossom mirror. The checksum is the same whichever you use. Prefer to wait until you've looked at the code? That's a sensible call, and the roadmap shows where things stand.
