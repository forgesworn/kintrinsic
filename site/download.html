Download

Get Kintrinsic.

Two pieces, and you only strictly need the first: the installer for your child's computer, and the app for your own phone. No account, no store, no tracking.

Start here · your child's computer · Linux

Kintrinsic for Linux 0.7.8

One installer: the warden, the lock screen and the Kintrinsic app — where you set up, adjust limits, connect a phone and recover access, all in one place. Installing changes nothing until you set them — no limits apply until you do. This alone is a complete, working setup.

Download the installer (.deb)

64-bit · about 11 MB · then in a terminal, from your Downloads folder: sudo apt install ./the-downloaded-file.deb

  • Linux Mint 22.x or another Ubuntu-based distro
  • A standard (non-admin) account for your child
  • A separate admin account for you
  • Five spare minutes — honestly

Optional · your phone · Android

Kintrinsic for Android 0.1.12

The parent app — for your phone, not your child's. Approve “more time”, get an alert the moment your child asks — even with the screen off — and change limits from anywhere. It talks to your family directly; there is no Google service in the middle.

Download Kintrinsic for Android

About 12 MB · Android 10+ and GrapheneOS · already have it? Installing over the top keeps your key and your family — nothing to set up again.

A note on the name under the hood. Everything you see says Kintrinsic — the apps, the installer, the menus. Only the terminal command and daemon (charterd) and the internal app id keep the earlier name charter — a deliberate, permanent internal name (much like Signal's package id). Same product, same keys.
  • Android 10 or newer — including GrapheneOS
  • No Google Play, no Google account
  • Reliable alerts with the screen off
  • Your key stays on your phone

Installing it on your phone

  1. Tap the download button above and let the file finish.
  2. Open the downloaded .apk file from your notifications or Files app.
  3. Android will ask whether to allow installing from this source. Allow it once, and the install proceeds.
Android will show you a warning, and that's normal. It appears for every app installed outside the Play Store — it is not a judgement about Kintrinsic specifically, and it is not a sign that anything is wrong. Android simply can't vouch for a file it didn't distribute.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

Because listing there would mean a Google developer account, Google's review of what your family is allowed to run, and — for the child-side app — permissions we've deliberately kept out of Google's hands. The whole point of Kintrinsic is that no company sits between you and your child's device, and that has to include us and them. So we hand you the file directly.

If your browser renames the file

Some browsers append .zip to downloads they don't recognise. If yours does, rename it so it ends in .apk and it will install normally. The file itself is unchanged.

Verifying this download (optional)

Checking a download's fingerprint confirms one thing: that the file you received is the file we meant to give you. It's a reasonable thing to want, and we'd rather you could.

We don't publish checksums yet. They're coming with the public source release, alongside the signing certificate fingerprint, so that what you verify against is something you can also inspect. Publishing a hash on the same page as the file it describes proves very little on its own — we'd rather ship the whole thing at once than the reassuring half of it.

Until then: the file is served over HTTPS from this domain and nowhere else. If you'd rather wait for verifiable builds before installing anything, that is a completely sensible call, and the roadmap tracks where it's up to.
