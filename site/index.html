Why Kintrinsic exists

Every other option asks you to spy on your own child.

The big parental-controls suites all work the same way: make an account, hand over your child's date of birth, and let a company hold a running record of where they went and what they looked at — so that you can be shown a dashboard of it. Safety and surveillance got bundled together, and parents were told that was the only shape it came in.

It isn't. A rule can live on the device, signed by you, and answer to nobody else. That's the whole idea.

✦

Private by architecture

No account, no vendor cloud, no dashboard on someone else's server. Rules travel sealed from your phone to the computer — we couldn't read your family's life if we wanted to.

☾

Humane by design

Ten-minute and one-minute warnings, never a mid-game cut-off. Apps are frozen where they stand, not killed, so nothing is lost — and a child can always ask for more time.

⚭

Works offline

The rules live on the device itself, signed by your key. Unplug the router, take the laptop to a caravan — the charter still holds, and requests catch up when it reconnects.

⌂

For the devices others ignore

The family Linux computer. The de-Googled phone. The machines every big parental-controls suite pretends don't exist are exactly where Kintrinsic lives.

How it works

Three steps, one evening.

You'll want a normal account for your child and a separate admin account for you — then:

Install on the child's computer

Download the installer, double-click, done. Nothing changes yet. Open Kintrinsic from the menu and, on its Home screen, pick your child's account — gentle default limits apply straight away.

Set the limits

In the same app, open The rules and set allowed hours and daily minutes. That's a complete setup on its own — no phone required, working within seconds.

Connect your phone

Point your phone's camera at the QR the computer shows, link your key, and from then on you change limits, see time used, and approve asks from the Kintrinsic app — anywhere.

Not sure you're technical enough? The Kintrinsic app has a “Copy this guide for your AI” button: it hands you a plain-text runbook you can paste into ChatGPT or Claude, which will then walk you through your own setup one step at a time, answering questions as you go.

Privacy

We can't read what doesn't exist.

  • No account. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to breach.
  • No location tracking. Not a feature we haven't built yet — one we refuse to build.
  • No browsing history uploaded. Reports are numbers — time used, requests made — never content.
  • No birthdate, no child profile. Your child is a name you chose and nothing more.

How that's possible: your phone holds a signing key — like a wax seal only you own. Every rule is sealed by that key, carried over an encrypted relay, and checked by the device against your seal alone.

The relay can't read it, can't forge it, and can't sell it. Your child's “please, five more minutes” travels sealed too — it never lands in anyone's log, including ours.

The source is open. Kintrinsic is published under the MIT licence — on GitHub, and heading for a decentralised home that isn't Microsoft's. Every download comes with checksums you can verify yourself. See the roadmap →

What you'll need

The honest hardware bit.

Kintrinsic has to be able to hold the line on the device itself, which means it can only live where the system lets it. Today that's Linux, and de-Googled Android in preview.

Ready today

Kintrinsic for Linux

One installer: the warden, the lock screen and the Kintrinsic app — where you set up, adjust limits, connect a phone and recover access. Installing changes nothing until you set up your child's account in it. If the family computer runs Linux Mint or Ubuntu, you're five minutes away.
