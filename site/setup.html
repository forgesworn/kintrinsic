Setup guide

Set up in one evening.

By the end of this page, your child's computer will lock itself outside the hours you chose, and give them a warning before their daily time runs out. That's a complete setup. The phone is optional and can wait.

Managing a child's phone rather than a computer? That's a different and longer job — it has its own guide.

Before you start

Kintrinsic keeps two roles apart, the same way your computer already does:

  • A standard account for your child — their everyday login, without admin rights.
  • A separate admin account for you — where you install and manage Kintrinsic.

This is the one part worth getting right. If your child can enter the admin password, Kintrinsic becomes a suggestion rather than an agreement — they could simply turn it off. Most family computers are already set up this way. If your child currently uses the admin account, make them a standard one first (System Settings → Users); it takes a minute and it's good practice regardless.

You'll need: a Linux Mint 22.x (or other Ubuntu-based) computer, your admin password, and about five minutes. No account to create, nothing to sign up for.

On the computer

1 · Install Kintrinsic

Download the installer and double-click it, or run it in a terminal:

sudo apt install ./the-downloaded-file.deb

Installing changes nothing yet — no limits apply until you set them. One package brings the warden (the part that keeps the charter), the lock screen, and the Kintrinsic app where you set up, adjust limits, connect a phone and recover access — all in one place.

Get the installer →

If that didn't work: a “package is of bad quality” message usually means the download was interrupted — fetch it again. If apt reports unmet dependencies, run sudo apt --fix-broken install and try once more.

2 · Choose your child's account

Open Kintrinsic from your applications menu — everything lives in this one app. On its Home screen, choose your child's account and press Set up Kintrinsic. Gentle default limits apply immediately — 7am to 8pm, two hours a day — so they're protected from the first minute. You can loosen or tighten them next.

If their account isn't listed: it's probably an administrator account, which Kintrinsic won't govern. Demote it in System Settings → Users first.

3 · Set the limits

In the same app, open The rules and set:

  • Allowed hours — the times of day the computer can be used. Set the whole week at once, then split out a day only if it really is different.
  • Daily time — a total per day, with 10-minute and 1-minute warnings before it's up.
  • Websites, apps & learning — optional: keep the web age-appropriate, block or time-limit a game, and mark learning sites as free so homework never costs playtime.

That is a complete, working setup. No phone required — it enforces even with the internet unplugged. Everything below is optional.

Connect your phone

This is optional, and you can do it any time. It's what lets you change limits, see time used, and approve “more time” asks from anywhere.

  1. On your phone, install the Kintrinsic app — no Google Play needed, and it gives you reliable alerts even with the screen off.
  2. Turn on parent approval, which creates your signing key on the phone.
  3. On the child's computer, open Kintrinsic and go to Connect a phone — it shows a QR code.
  4. Point your phone's camera at it. That links your key to the computer. Done — you now manage it from the sofa or the school run.

If the scan doesn't take: there's an offline path — the Kintrinsic app's parent-approval card shows a pairing link you can type in by hand. No internet needed on either device.

Back up your key. Kintrinsic offers an encrypted backup of your signing key when you pair. Save it somewhere safe — it's how you get back in if you ever lose your phone. This is the one step people skip and later wish they hadn't.

If something goes wrong

You lose your phone, or your key

Your child's limits keep working regardless — they live on the device. To manage again, restore your key from the encrypted backup you saved, and reconnect. On the computer itself, open Kintrinsic and go to Recovery — an admin-only way back in. This is exactly why the backup step matters — do it once, keep it safe.

You need everything off, right now

Your admin account is never governed by Kintrinsic, so you always have a way in. Open Kintrinsic and go to Recovery — it offers Pause, Resume and Turn off. If you'd rather do it from a terminal, sudo systemctl stop charterd.service stops enforcement and unfreezes anything currently frozen.

Something looks wrong and you're not sure what

Run charter status in a terminal — it prints what Kintrinsic thinks is going on: whether it's paired, which account it's governing, and what the limits currently are.
