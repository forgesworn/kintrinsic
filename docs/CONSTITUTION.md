# The Charter Constitution

*The short list every Charter decision gets checked against. If a feature,
default, or line of copy can't be reconciled with these, the feature is wrong —
not the constitution.*

Charter is **companion parenting technology, not control technology.** It helps a
family agree how screens fit their life, keep to that agreement, reflect on how
it's going, and — the goal — outgrow the need for the tool at all. Everything
below follows from that.

---

## 1. Companion, not control

The relationship is **wardship by agreement**, not containment. We design for a
family that mostly wants the same thing, not an adversary to be outwitted.

- We do **not** design around circumvention. A child moving to another device
  when time's up is not a breach to seal — it's visible, and a conversation.
  Enforcement *hardness* is never the goal; a **legible, shared agreement** is.
- The child always knows where they stand (the ward-side mirror). Transparency to
  the ward is the mechanism that makes it an agreement rather than a trap — there
  is little to "game" about a rule you can see and understand.
- Copy is warm and plain. The lock screen offers a way to ask, and always a way
  to call for help. Enforcement is the floor, not the personality.

## 2. The family owns the data. Full stop.

Whatever Charter knows about a family belongs to that family — not to us, not to
anyone.

- This is **true by construction, not by promise**: no cloud, no company copy,
  the keys and the data live on the family's own devices.
- What a family collects, how they read it, and who they choose to share it with
  (a partner, a grandparent, a psychologist) is **their** call. We do not know
  their circumstances, and deciding the "right" way to use their own data *for*
  them would just be authoritarianism aimed at the parent instead of the child.
- "Surveillance" vs "monitoring" is a false distinction — the same act differs
  only by who holds the data and why. We don't legislate the word; we hold the
  invariant below.

## 3. Transparency is the invariant we build; the rest is the family's call

We hold three things firmly and leave the rest to the family:

- **We are never the covert party.** No harvesting, no hidden capability, no
  phoning home. The tool is honest — to everyone — about what it can and does do.
- **It is a genuine choice, not a silent default.** Reflection, tracking, tighter
  or looser rules are things a family turns *toward* when it serves them, and
  away from when it doesn't.
- **We never deceive anyone about what it does.**

> **Open call (decented to set):** "transparent" can mean *system-honest* (Charter
> never acts hidden — the hard invariant) and/or *ward-visible* (the child sees
> their own data). Proposed: system-honesty is non-negotiable; ward-visibility is
> a strong default the family can shape, because a household facing something
> serious — or working with a professional — may legitimately need it configured
> differently, and that isn't ours to forbid.

## 4. Charter aims to make itself unnecessary

Success is **declining reliance**, not engagement or retention.

- This is **structural, not aspirational.** Free, open-source, no cloud, no
  per-user revenue, nothing to harvest — so "we want you to need us less" isn't a
  temptation we resist; there is no temptation. (Every incumbent has a revenue
  line that's some function of retention, so they *cannot afford to mean it* —
  the Weight Watchers trap, where a permanently-changed customer is a lost one.)
- The model is a **good teacher's**: the aim is the student not needing you, and
  there is always a next student. "Unnecessary per family, once they've
  graduated" and "serves households for years" are not in tension.
- **Open source makes the promise un-betrayable.** Good intentions get acquired;
  a future owner chasing engagement can't quietly enshittify a tool families
  already hold and can fork. Same move as keeping the keys on the device: don't
  ask to be trusted — remove the ability to betray.
- In practice: **graduation is a first-class, celebrated moment**; there are **no
  re-engagement nudges**; "turn it off / we're done here" is a **proud exit**, not
  a buried one. Metrics of success are goals met and wind-downs completed — never
  time-in-app.

*Public stance:* **the only screen-time tool built to want you to stop needing
it.** No competitor can copy that without blowing up their own model.

## On data & reflection (how §1–§4 combine)

Charter can help a family *see and shape* their week — trends over time, a
**wind-down plan** (e.g. easing 6h/day toward 1h/day over weeks), the way a
smartwatch helps build a habit and then becomes irrelevant. This is **reflection,
not policing**:

- **Eventual reconciliation, not real-time enforcement.** Each device counts
  locally (offline is fine); the picture reconciles when devices reconnect. This
  is easy and honest — it's real-time cross-device *policing* that we decline, not
  cross-device *understanding*.
- **An overdraft, not a slammed door.** Going over the agreed time shows as the
  red part of the week's bar, to reflect on and wind back — it might be gaming, a
  rabbit hole, or simply losing track of time the way anyone loses track of money
  some months. Charter's job is to make the week *visible*, not to lock the drawer.
- **The ward sees their own trends** by default (the smartwatch model — your data
  is yours), subject to the §3 open call.
